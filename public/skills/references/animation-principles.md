# 12 Principles of Animation (Disney / Pixar)

1. **Squash and Stretch** — Gives sense of weight, volume, flexibility and impact. Essential for physicality and emotional exaggeration (face, body).

2. **Anticipation** — Prepares the audience for an action. Builds expectation and makes the main action clearer and more powerful.

3. **Staging** — Clear presentation of an idea, pose or emotion. Silhouette, contrast, composition so the emotion reads instantly.

4. **Straight Ahead Action and Pose-to-Pose** — Two approaches to drawing frames. Pose-to-pose for controlled emotional beats; straight ahead for spontaneous energy.

5. **Follow Through and Overlapping Action** — Different parts of the body (or clothing, hair) continue moving after the main action stops. Creates natural flow and life.

6. **Slow In and Slow Out (Ease In / Ease Out)** — Acceleration and deceleration. Makes movement feel organic and weighted.

7. **Arcs** — Most natural movement follows curved paths. Straight lines feel mechanical.

8. **Secondary Action** — Supporting actions that reinforce the primary emotion or action without competing (hand gestures, eye darts, weight shifts, ear flicks).

9. **Timing** — Number of frames determines weight, personality and emotional intensity. Critical for comedy and drama.

10. **Exaggeration** — Push beyond realism for clarity and emotional impact while keeping the truth of the feeling. Essential in animation for readable emotions.

11. **Solid Drawing** — Sense of three-dimensional form, volume and weight even in 2D. Avoids flat or “twisted” forms.

12. **Appeal** — Charisma and design that makes the character interesting and watchable. Not the same as “cute” — villains can have strong appeal.

## Application to Emotion & Mimicry
- Root exaggeration in real facial anatomy and basic emotions (Ekman: joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust).
- Use Staging + Secondary Action + Timing so the emotion is readable in a single frame (silhouette test).
- For dialogue: secondary actions (eyebrow raises, head tilts, hand emphasis) sell the internal state.
- In cartoons: push further than live-action while preserving psychological truth so the audience still empathizes.
