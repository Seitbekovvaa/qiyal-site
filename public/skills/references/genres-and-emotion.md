# Genre Conventions & Emotional Psychology

## Core Genre Expectations

**Comedy**
- Setup → expectation → reversal (punchline). Timing is everything.
- Escalation of absurdity, callbacks, running gags, fish-out-of-water.
- High-key lighting, energetic pacing, physical + verbal comedy.
- Fun and Games section delivers the promise of the premise.

**Romance / Romantic Comedy**
- Meet-cute (chemistry + conflict).
- Obstacles (internal flaws + external).
- Midpoint of intimacy or complication.
- Dark moment / breakup.
- Grand gesture and reunion / HEA.
- Warm soft lighting, intimate close-ups, score that swells with feeling.

**Drama**
- Character-driven internal conflict, moral dilemmas, relationships.
- Emotional truth and subtext over spectacle.
- Naturalistic or low-key lighting, longer takes, close-ups that reveal internal life.

**Horror**
- Build dread (terror of the unknown) → release (horror payoff).
- Isolation, vulnerability, rules of the monster (or breaking them).
- Low-key / chiaroscuro, Dutch angles, silence + sudden stingers.
- Sound design is the primary fear engine.

**Action**
- Clear visual geography, escalating set-pieces, ticking clock.
- Physical stakes, hero vs superior force.
- Dynamic camera (tracking, handheld, crane), readable fast cuts.

**Fantasy**
- Clear rules of magic / world.
- Wonder + character emotion (often Hero’s Journey).
- Epic scale, saturated or ethereal color depending on tone.

**Psychological Thriller**
- Paranoia, unreliable perception, mind games, gaslighting.
- Subjective camera, distorted sound and light, micro-expressions in close-up.
- Twists that reframe everything the audience thought they knew.

**Detective / Mystery**
- Fair clues + clever red herrings.
- Investigation structure, withheld information.
- Atmospheric lighting, focus on the detective’s process and reveal.

## Emotional Psychology for Screen
- Basic universal emotions (Ekman): joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust.
- Micro-expressions are fleeting and involuntary — use Extreme Close-Up to catch them.
- Subtext: the gap between what is said and what the body/eyes do.
- Body language, posture, proxemics, fidgeting reveal status and internal state.
- Sound and music prime the emotional state before the image fully lands.
- Audience uses predictive processing — violate or fulfill expectations for surprise, fear or satisfaction.
- Mirror neurons and identification create empathy; give the audience a clear emotional window through the protagonist.
