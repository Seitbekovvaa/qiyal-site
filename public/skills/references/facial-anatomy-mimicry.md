# Anatomy of Facial Expressions (Mimicry)

Facial expressions are produced by the coordinated contraction of mimetic muscles (all innervated by the facial nerve, CN VII). The most rigorous system for describing them is the **Facial Action Coding System (FACS)** developed by Paul Ekman and Wallace Friesen. FACS decomposes any visible facial movement into **Action Units (AUs)** — the smallest observable units of muscle action.

## Key Muscle Groups

**Orbital (around the eyes)**
- *Orbicularis oculi* (AU6, AU7) — closes the eye, raises the cheek, creates crow’s feet
- *Corrugator supercilii* + *Depressor supercilii* (AU4) — pulls brows down and together (frown lines)
- *Frontalis* (AU1, AU2) — raises inner or outer brows
- *Levator palpebrae superioris* (AU5) — raises the upper eyelid

**Nasal**
- *Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi* + *Procerus* (AU9) — wrinkles the nose
- *Nasalis* — flares or compresses the nostrils

**Oral (mouth and lips)**
- *Zygomaticus major* (AU12) — pulls lip corners up and back (the main “smile” muscle)
- *Depressor anguli oris* (AU15) — pulls lip corners down
- *Orbicularis oris* — purses or closes the lips
- *Mentalis* (AU17) — raises the chin and lower lip
- *Risorius* + *Platysma* (AU20) — stretches the lips laterally
- *Levator labii superioris* (AU10) — raises the upper lip

## Basic Emotions and Their Typical Action Units

| Emotion     | Core Action Units                  | Visible Changes |
|-------------|------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Happiness / Joy | 6 + 12                            | Raised cheeks, crow’s feet, lip corners pulled up (Duchenne smile when both are present) |
| Sadness     | 1 + 4 + 15                        | Inner brows raised and drawn together, lip corners down |
| Surprise    | 1 + 2 + 5 + 26                    | Brows fully raised, upper lids raised, jaw dropped |
| Fear        | 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 7 + 20 + 26      | Brows raised and drawn, eyes wide and tense, lips stretched, jaw drop |
| Anger       | 4 + 5 + 7 + 23                    | Brows lowered and drawn, lids tensed, lips pressed |
| Disgust     | 9 + 10 + 17                       | Nose wrinkled, upper lip raised, chin raised |
| Contempt    | Unilateral 12 or 14               | One-sided lip corner raise or dimpler |

Real expressions are almost never pure. Intensity, asymmetry and secondary AUs vary by individual, culture and context.

## Important Physiological Realities

- **Asymmetry** is normal and increases with genuine emotion or age.
- **Skin behavior**: muscles pull the skin, creating folds, wrinkles and stretching. Subsurface scattering and blood flow change with emotion (blushing, paling).
- **Micro-expressions** last 1/25–1/5 of a second and often leak true affect.
- **Age** deepens permanent lines (glabellar, nasolabial, crow’s feet) that become more visible under expression.
- **Eyes** carry enormous weight: the difference between a polite smile (AU12 only) and a genuine Duchenne smile (AU6+12) is mainly in the eyes.

## Application to Character Design & Animation

**Realism**
- Respect muscle origins, insertions and the way skin slides over them.
- Preserve individual facial identity (bone structure, resting folds, asymmetry) even under strong expression.
- Light reveals the forms created by the muscles (nasolabial fold deepens, cheeks push up, etc.).

**Stylization**
- Exaggerate the same muscle logic rather than inventing arbitrary shapes.
- Pixar / Disney: clarify and amplify the key AUs while keeping appeal and readability.
- Arcane / painterly styles: push the emotional intensity of the same muscle groups with richer surface detail.
- Anime: often extreme eye and brow AUs; the underlying logic still maps to real muscle actions.
- Stop-motion / Corpse Bride: mechanical exaggeration of the same anatomical principles.

When analyzing a reference or designing an expression, identify the active Action Units first, then decide how strongly and how stylistically to show them. This keeps expressions both anatomically grounded and emotionally legible.
