# Camera Direction (Режиссура камеры)

Camera is not a recording device — it is a storytelling instrument. Every choice of size, angle, movement and lens carries meaning.

## Shot Sizes and Their Emotional Weight

| Shot Size              | Abbreviation | What it communicates |
|------------------------|--------------|----------------------|
| Extreme Wide / Extreme Long | EWS / ELS   | Scale, isolation, geography, character vs world |
| Wide / Long Shot       | WS / LS      | Full body + environment, relationships, blocking |
| Medium Shot            | MS           | Waist-up, conversation, action with context |
| Medium Close-Up        | MCU          | Chest-up, intimacy without claustrophobia |
| Close-Up               | CU           | Face, emotion, key reaction |
| Extreme Close-Up       | ECU          | Eyes, detail, heightened intensity or object |

## Camera Angles

- **Eye-level** — neutral, equal, invisible. Default for most dialogue.
- **Low angle** — power, dominance, heroism, threat. Subject looks larger.
- **High angle** — vulnerability, weakness, observation, sometimes judgment.
- **Dutch / Canted** — unease, instability, psychological disturbance.
- **Over-the-shoulder (OTS)** — conversation, connection, point-of-view adjacency.
- **POV** — subjective experience of a character.

## Camera Movements

**Motivated** (preferred): the camera moves because something in the scene causes or justifies the movement (character walks → tracking; revelation → slow push-in).

**Unmotivated**: pure style. Use sparingly and with intention.

Common movements:
- **Static / Locked-off** — observational, formal, tension through stillness
- **Pan / Tilt** — reveal, follow, shift attention
- **Dolly / Tracking** — move with or toward subject (intimacy, journey, pursuit)
- **Push-in / Pull-out** — increase or release emotional pressure
- **Crane / Boom** — vertical power, scale, transition
- **Orbit / Arc** — emphasize character or transformation
- **Handheld** — urgency, documentary feel, instability (must feel intentional)
- **Steadicam / Gimbal** — smooth, continuous, floating presence

One clear intention per shot is almost always stronger than multiple simultaneous moves.

## Continuity Rules

- **180-degree rule**: keep the camera on one side of the axis between two characters (or along the line of action). Crossing the line reverses screen direction and can disorient.
- **30-degree rule**: change angle by at least 30° between shots of the same subject to avoid jump cuts.
- Eyeline match and screen direction must be consistent unless deliberately broken for psychological effect.

Breaking these rules is a powerful tool when done intentionally (disorientation, power shift, psychological break).

## Practical Application for AI Video & Storyboarding

When writing prompts or building a storyboard:
1. Choose shot size for emotional distance.
2. Choose angle for power relationship.
3. Choose one motivated camera movement (or static).
4. Specify lens feel if relevant (24 mm wide / 50 mm natural / 85 mm intimate).
5. Respect or consciously break continuity rules.
6. Prefer hard cuts between clearly defined shots over morphs.

Camera direction is the invisible performance that shapes how the audience feels every moment.
