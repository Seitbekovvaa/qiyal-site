# Style Encyclopedia & Analysis Framework

## Major Studio & Film Styles

**Pixar**
- Balanced, appealing proportions with a characteristic "golden" harmony.
- High tactile realism in materials, light and physics while remaining stylized.
- Emotional performance driven by eyes and subtle acting rather than extreme squash.
- Clean, readable silhouettes and strong design clarity.

**Disney (classic & modern)**
- Classic: strong appeal, larger head-to-body ratios, clear storytelling shapes, high saturation.
- Modern: more cinematic lighting and proportion systems while retaining strong silhouette and expression readability.

**DreamWorks**
- Flexible "no single house style". Ranges from satirical (Shrek), graphic novel (Bad Guys), painterly (Wild Robot) to storybook/painterly-anime hybrid (Puss in Boots: The Last Wish).
- Strong push poses and character acting.

**Illumination**
- Simplified, rounded, high-appeal forms.
- Bold color, comic energy, readable shapes designed for broad audience clarity.

**Arcane (Fortiche)**
- Painterly 2D/3D hybrid with rich surface detail and dramatic lighting.
- Strong silhouettes, clothing and armor that express personality and status.
- Faces carry high emotional intensity; designs evolve with character arcs.

**Puss in Boots: The Last Wish**
- "Fairy tale painting" aesthetic — storybook illustration quality with painterly depth.
- Anime-influenced action moments mixed with more traditional emotional acting.
- Graphic rims, edge breakup and selective stylization for impact.

**Corpse Bride / Tim Burton stop-motion**
- Elongated gothic proportions.
- Strong contrast between the living (more muted, restrained) and the dead (more colorful, expressive).
- Intricate facial mechanics and Victorian/calavera influences.

**Anime spectrum**
- Wide range: soft atmospheric (Ghibli), dynamic and graphic (Trigger etc.), more realistic modern approaches.
- Key variables: eye size and shape language, line weight variation, color blocking vs soft rendering, degree of motion exaggeration.

## Style Analysis Checklist (use on any reference)

1. **Proportion system** — head-to-body ratio, limb length, eye size relative to face, overall scale language.
2. **Shape language** — dominant shapes (circles = soft/friendly, triangles = aggressive/dynamic, rectangles = stable/strong).
3. **Line quality** — clean/vector, variable weight, sketchy, broken, calligraphic.
4. **Facial feature stylization** — nose size and shape, eye shape and spacing, lip thickness, jaw and brow design, distinctive non-standard traits.
5. **Color approach** — saturation level, palette temperature, how local color shifts under light, limited vs rich palette.
6. **Shading & texture** — cel, soft gradient, painterly strokes, high-frequency detail, material emphasis.
7. **Silhouette & readability** — how well the character reads in pure black/white.
8. **Distinctive "signature" quirks** that make the style immediately recognizable.

When adapting a character into a new style, lock the identity anchors (key proportions, signature features, personality expression) and translate only the stylistic layer.
