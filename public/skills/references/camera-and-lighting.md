# Cinematography, Lighting, Color & Sound

## Shot Sizes
- Extreme Wide / Establishing: location, scale, isolation of character
- Wide / Long / Full: full body + environment, action, physical relationships
- Medium: waist-up, dialogue, interaction
- Medium Close-Up: chest-up, emotional conversation
- Close-Up: face, emotion, reaction, empathy
- Extreme Close-Up: eyes, hands, object, detail for intensity or symbolism

## Camera Angles
- Eye-level: neutral, observational
- Low angle: power, dominance, threat, heroism
- High angle: vulnerability, weakness, judgment, smallness
- Dutch / Canted: instability, madness, unease, disorientation
- Over-the-shoulder (OTS): conversation, relationship dynamics
- POV: subjective immersion
- Bird’s-eye / Overhead: scale, fate, isolation, pattern

## Camera Movements
- Static: contemplation, power, tension through stillness
- Pan / Tilt: reveal, follow, hierarchy
- Dolly / Tracking / Push-in: intimacy, rising tension, realization
- Pull-out: isolation, reveal of larger context
- Steadicam: elegant immersion, continuous following
- Handheld: urgency, chaos, realism, anxiety
- Crane / Jib: epic scale, dramatic reveal
- Dolly Zoom (Vertigo): psychological shock, disorientation, vertigo

## Lighting
- Three-point: Key (main), Fill (soften shadows), Back/Rim (separation)
- Motivated: light justified by practical sources in the scene
- High-key: bright, soft shadows → comedy, romance, optimism
- Low-key / Chiaroscuro: strong contrast, deep shadows → drama, noir, horror, mystery
- Color temperature: Warm (~3200K tungsten) intimacy/nostalgia; Cool (~5600K) clinical/sad/alienating

## Color Theory (see also references/color-theory.md for full deep dive)

Color is a primary emotional language. Key principles:

- Hue, Saturation, Value control mood more powerfully than most dialogue.
- Warm colors (red/orange/yellow) advance, energize, create intimacy or danger.
- Cool colors (blue/green/purple) recede, calm, isolate or create melancholy/technology feel.
- Complementary schemes (especially teal & orange) create maximum contrast and make skin tones pop.
- Monochromatic and limited palettes strengthen thematic control.
- Color scripts (Pixar method) map emotional progression across the entire film.
- Grading in post can shift entire emotional arcs (desaturate for depression, warm for hope, etc.).

## Sound Design Psychology
- Sound reaches emotional processing faster than image
- Diegetic (in world) vs Non-diegetic (score, narration)
- Foley + ambient = immersion and reality
- Score: tempo, harmony/dissonance, volume primes emotion
- Horror toolkit: low-frequency drones, silence before stingers, scream-like textures (amygdala)
- Silence is powerful
- Irony: happy music over tragic image (or reverse)
- Walter Murch’s Law of 2½: limit simultaneous layers
- Always define sonic intention of the scene
