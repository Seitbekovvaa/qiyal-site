# Realism, Clothing, Light & Space

## Realistic Human Depiction

**Skin**
- Subsurface scattering (strongest on ears, nostrils, fingertips, thin areas).
- Natural pore density variation (higher in T-zone).
- Slight facial asymmetry, micro-blemishes, freckles, vellus hair.
- Variation in roughness (lips and eyelids smoother, forehead and cheeks more textured).
- Color variation: blood flow, pigmentation, cool/warm undertones.

**Hair**
- Multiple layers and strand groups, natural clumping.
- Anisotropic specular highlights that follow strand direction.
- Translucency and rim lighting on edges.
- Realistic attachment to scalp and volume behavior under gravity/motion.

**Fabric & Clothing**
- Folds determined by body topology + gravity + material stiffness.
- Material-specific response: matte cotton, soft specular silk, structured leather, heavy wool.
- Micro-texture of weave, seams, wear, aging.
- How light wraps, creates contact shadows and reveals form.

**Light Behavior**
- Daylight: direction and color temperature change with time of day (warm golden hour, cool overcast, hard midday).
- Practical lamp light: warm, localized, strong falloff, soft or hard depending on source size.
- Night: cool ambient + practicals, higher contrast, color from artificial sources.
- Always consider how light interacts with the specific material (skin SSS, fabric specular, metal reflection).

**Poses & Physiology**
- Weight distribution and centers of gravity.
- Natural asymmetry and counter-balance.
- Muscle tension and joint limits.
- How the body actually sits, leans, reaches and rests.

**Facial Expressions**
- Based on real facial musculature and Action Units.
- Emotion creates specific folds, crow’s feet, nasolabial changes, asymmetry.
- Preserve the character’s recognizable features even under strong expression.
- Micro-expressions and residual tension matter for believability.

## Clothing & Costume Sense

- Situational correctness (home, work, formal, weather, social status, personality).
- Silhouette and proportion as character expression.
- Color harmony with skin, hair and environment under the given lighting.
- Fabric behavior must match the material and the motion/pose.
- Lived-in details: wrinkles from wear, slight imperfections, personality through accessories and fit.

## Space & Environment

- Clear sense of scale relative to the human figure.
- Depth layers, leading lines, atmospheric perspective.
- Material authenticity and how light reveals texture (wood grain, plaster, metal, fabric, concrete).
- Practical details that make a space feel inhabited: wear, clutter, light sources, history.
- Time of day and lighting completely transform the mood and color of the same location.
- Studio vs home vs street vs fantastical spaces each have distinct visual languages and constraints.
