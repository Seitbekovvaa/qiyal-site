# Working with the Operator (DP) and Blocking

## What is Blocking

Blocking is the precise choreography of:
- Where actors stand and how they move
- Where the camera is placed and how it moves
- How lighting supports both

It is the three-dimensional staging of the scene before any film is shot. Good blocking tells story and psychology through space, distance, height and movement without needing extra dialogue.

## Director – DP Collaboration

**Director** owns:
- Performance and emotional intention
- Overall blocking of actors
- Shot selection and emotional purpose of each shot
- Final say on what is covered

**Director of Photography (DP / Cinematographer)** owns:
- How the image is captured (lighting, lenses, exposure, camera movement quality)
- Technical feasibility and visual beauty of the director’s intention
- Collaboration on camera positions once actor blocking is set

Typical healthy process:
1. Director works with actors first (sometimes privately) to find truthful movement and emotional beats.
2. DP watches or joins and begins planning camera positions, lenses and lighting.
3. Joint “stopping rehearsal” — actors move through the scene, stopping at key positions so marks can be placed (tape on the floor for actors and camera).
4. Lighting and camera are set to the marks.
5. Technical rehearsal with full crew.
6. Shooting.

Some directors prefer the DP present from the first blocking; others want private time with actors first. Both approaches work when communication is clear.

## Key Elements of Strong Blocking

- **Power and relationship** through distance, height and who moves toward whom.
- **Eyelines** carefully matched for continuity and emotional connection.
- **Camera motivation** — the camera should feel like it is responding to the actors or the story, not decorating.
- **Marks** — exact positions for actors and camera so focus, lighting and framing stay consistent across takes.
- **Coverage plan** — which sizes and angles will be shot, and in what order (often wide first, then tighter).

## Practical Application for AI Video & Storyboarding

When designing a scene or writing multi-shot prompts:

1. Decide the emotional geography of the scene first (who has power, who is vulnerable, how distance changes).
2. Stage the actors in space.
3. Place the camera relative to that staging (shot size + angle + one motivated movement).
4. Describe both actor movement and camera movement clearly in the prompt or storyboard panel.
5. Keep continuity of screen direction and eyelines across shots.

Example language:
“Character A starts stage left, walks slowly toward Character B who remains still center frame. Camera begins in medium wide, slowly dollies in and slightly arcs to end in medium close-up on A’s face as they stop. Eye-level, 35 mm feel. Hard cut.”

Blocking is where performance, space and camera become one storytelling unit.
