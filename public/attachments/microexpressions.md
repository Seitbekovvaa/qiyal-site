# Microexpressions Analysis

Microexpressions are very brief, involuntary facial expressions that reveal a person’s true emotional state, often when they are trying to conceal or suppress it.

## Definition & Duration

- Typical duration: **1/25 to 1/5 of a second** (≈ 40–200 ms).  
  Some research extends the upper limit to ≤ 500 ms (total duration) or onset ≤ 260 ms.
- They are significantly shorter than ordinary (macro) expressions, which usually last 0.5–4 seconds.
- Because they are so fast, they are difficult to spot with the naked eye without training.

## Key Characteristics

- **Involuntary** — they cannot be fully controlled or prevented.
- **Low intensity** — usually weaker than full expressions.
- **Leakage** of true affect (especially when a person is lying, suppressing emotion, or under high cognitive load).
- Can appear on the whole face or be limited to one region (eyes, brows, mouth).
- Based on the same facial musculature and Action Units (FACS) as full expressions.

## The Seven Universal Microexpressions (Ekman)

They correspond to the same basic emotions and Action Units as full expressions:

| Emotion     | Typical AUs              | Notes |
|-------------|--------------------------|-------|
| Happiness   | 6 + 12                  | Duchenne markers (crow’s feet) may appear briefly |
| Sadness     | 1 + 4 + 15              | Inner brow raise is particularly diagnostic |
| Anger       | 4 + 5 + 7 + 23          | Brow lowering + lid tightening |
| Fear        | 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 7 + 20  | Wide eyes + stretched lips |
| Surprise    | 1 + 2 + 5 + 26          | Brows raised + jaw drop |
| Disgust     | 9 + 10                  | Nose wrinkle is highly reliable |
| Contempt    | Unilateral 12 or 14     | One-sided lip corner raise |

## Reliable vs Controllable Muscles

Ekman noted that certain muscles are harder to control consciously (“reliable muscles”).  
When these fire involuntarily, the microexpression is more trustworthy as a signal of true emotion (e.g., the orbital part of *orbicularis oculi* in a genuine smile).

## Detection & Reliability

- Untrained observers detect only about 40–50% of microexpressions.
- With proper training (e.g., Ekman’s METT), accuracy can rise above 80%.
- Context, cultural display rules, and individual differences affect both production and recognition.
- Microexpressions of negative emotions have been shown to help differentiate truth-tellers from those concealing malicious intent in experimental settings.

## Application in Character Design & Animation

**Realism**
- Adding correctly timed, low-intensity microexpressions dramatically increases the sense of psychological depth and authenticity.
- They are especially powerful in moments of internal conflict, deception, or suppressed emotion.

**Stylization**
- In stylized characters (Pixar, Arcane, anime, etc.) microexpressions can be slightly prolonged or clarified so the audience can still register them, while remaining true to the underlying Action Units.
- Even a single frame or two of the “leaked” expression is often enough if the key AUs are readable.

**Practical workflow**
1. Decide the character’s true internal emotion.
2. Decide the emotion they are trying to display.
3. Insert a brief (1–5 frames at 24 fps) activation of the true emotion’s Action Units.
4. Return quickly to the masked expression.
5. Keep intensity low and respect the character’s unique facial structure.

Microexpressions are one of the highest-leverage tools for making a character feel psychologically real.
